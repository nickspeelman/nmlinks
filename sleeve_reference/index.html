Butcherbird, thorns & lichen.

Reference material for a sleeve section combining a loggerhead shrike, honey locust, and bark lichens. The page focuses on anatomy, hunting behavior, thorn structure, bark texture, and lichen color.

01 / the bird

Shrike hunting and impaling behavior.

Loggerhead shrikes hunt from exposed perches and take insects, mice, lizards, small birds, and other prey. They have a hooked bill but relatively weak songbird feet rather than raptorial talons.

Larger prey may be impaled on thorns, sharp twigs, or barbed wire, or wedged into branch forks. These food caches are called larders. They are used for feeding and storage and may also play a role in mate attraction.

U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service specifically lists honey locust as an example of a thorny tree used by loggerhead shrikes for impaling prey.

02 / the tree

Honey locust structure and thorns.

Wild honey locust (Gleditsia triacanthos) can produce long, rigid, branching thorns directly from trunks and limbs. Mature bark forms rough gray-brown ridges. The tree also has fine compound foliage and long, often twisted seedpods.

Shrikes do not depend on honey locust specifically. They use many thorny shrubs, trees, and artificial sharp points. Honey locust provides suitable rigid thorns and occurs in open habitats used by loggerhead shrikes.

03 / lichen notes

Lichens on honey locust.

Lichens are symbiotic organisms in which a fungus forms the main body and a photosynthetic partner — an alga and/or cyanobacterium — supplies energy. Bark-growing lichens use the tree primarily as a substrate and are not parasites drawing nutrients from the tree.

A Chicago-area survey recorded 20 lichen species on honey locust, including the species highlighted below. These provide documented yellow, orange, green, and gray color references for honey-locust bark.

Candleflame / lemon

Candelaria concolor

Lemon to gold; small, frilly foliose patches.

Common greenshield

Flavoparmelia caperata

Broad yellow-green lobes that can form relatively large patches.

Hooded sunburst

Xanthomendoza fallax

Yellow to orange foliose growth with a warmer color range.

Orange-cored shadow

Phaeophyscia rubropulchra

Gray-green outer surface with a vivid orange-red inner layer.

Mealy rosette

Physcia millegrana

Pale gray foliose rosettes that contrast with darker bark.
